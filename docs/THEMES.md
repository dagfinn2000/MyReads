## Choosing a theme

- 192 colour themes, 87 light, 87 dark and the Silly set, or a light
  and a dark one that switch with your
  device, with an accent colour of your own on top if you like. Your
  choice follows you to every device you sign in on, unless you give one
  phone or computer a theme of its own ("Only on this device" in the
  palette menu). The palette menu sorts them by kind: Plain, Easy
  reading, Glass, Colour pairs, Design and craft, Paper and print,
  Scenes, Desktops and screens, and the Silly set last, with the light
  ones of each kind before the dark. It shows each theme as a small round
  swatch, the page colour with the accent as a dot (and the theme's
  picture or outline, if it has one): point at one to see its name (and,
  for some, a word about it), or tap it to try it. No two themes look alike: fourteen that were too
  close to others were retired, and so were Sea Glass, Contour and
  Leadlight, whose pictures crowded the page. If you had one of them you
  now have the theme nearest to it (Porcelain, Ink Wash and Material You
  for those three).

## Filling the gaps

- Sixteen more fill the gaps the others left, eight light and eight dark:
  - For easy reading. Colour Safe Light and Colour Safe Dark are for
    colour blindness, in the Okabe-Ito colours that read the same to
    every kind of colour vision: blue for what is chosen and orange or
    yellow for focus, never red against green. Status badges, moods and
    charts follow (greens turn bluish green and reds vermilion), links
    in text are underlined and focus is a solid outline, so nothing
    depends on colour alone. Night Light is for reading in bed: amber on
    near black, with no blue in any colour on the page, and covers,
    badges, charts and the reading room turned amber too. Sepia Light is
    Sepia's warm e-reader page in light, everything in the book face.
  - The other side of a theme that only came in one mode. Light:
    Terminal Light (dark green type on a paper-white screen, with
    Terminal's prompt, cursor, double-lined boxes and bracketed buttons),
    Whiteprint (Blueprint's blue lines on white squared paper, with its
    lettering, dimension lines and title block), Whiteboard (a glossy
    white board catching the window's light, headings in marker, green
    marker for what is chosen and the board's aluminium frame for the
    top bar) and Art Deco Light (ivory card stock, midnight blue and
    gold, every card framed twice in gold). Dark: E-Paper Dark (an
    e-reader in dark mode, no colour and no shadows), Letterpress Dark
    (black cotton card printed in cream, every card pressed into it),
    Newsprint Dark (a newspaper's night edition: charcoal newsprint with
    all of Newsprint's masthead, rules, halftones and coupons) and
    Notebook Dark (a black notebook written in white gel pen, with
    Notebook's margin, holes, tape and sticky notes).
  - More glass, so that both modes have each colour of glass. Light:
    Jade (pale green glass in daylight, cinnabar red for what is chosen)
    and Amber (amber glass in the sun with a honey glow low in the
    corner, deep teal). Dark: Garnet (rose glass on a red so deep it is
    nearly black, pale rose gold) and Crystal (clear glass in a dark
    room, with a faint band of rainbow light across the corner and the
    spectrum along the top of every pane).
## Forty more

- Forty more, some of every kind, seventeen light, seventeen dark and six
  silly:
  - Plain: Melon (a warm apricot page, burnt orange for what is chosen)
    and Oxblood (the deep red-brown of old leather, each card a little
    lighter, pale coral for what is chosen).
  - For easy reading. Hyperlegible and Hyperlegible Dark set every word
    in Atkinson Hyperlegible, the face the Braille Institute drew so that
    letters easily mixed up (I, l and 1; O and 0; b, d, p and q) look

    outline on whatever the keyboard is on. The light one is a cool white
    that doesn't glare, the dark one a soft charcoal, never pure black,

    reeded glass, its fine upright ribs catching the light, over coral

## Two forests

- Two forests, the one scene that fills the whole window, with things
  in it that move:
  - Wildwood (dark): deep in a forest at night. Old trunks crowd in from
    the edges and stand back into the dark, moonlight comes down through
    the leaves in pale shafts, mist lies low among the ferns, mushrooms
    glow faintly at the roots, and fireflies drift between the trees,
    each lighting up now and then. Gold for what is chosen.
  - Birchwood (light): a birch wood on a sunny morning. White trunks
    marked in black, sunlight slanting down through fresh green leaves,
    ferns and wood anemones along the foot, and now and then a leaf
    coming down. Spruce green for what is chosen. The nearest birches keep
    to the margins beside the page, so their black marks never sit under
    writing.
  - Cards in both are a little see-through, so the forest stays behind
    them. With animations off (Preferences, or the device asking for less
    motion), or Moving backgrounds off, the fireflies glow where they are
    and the leaves hang still.

## Themes that restyle the whole app

- **Thirty-six themes restyle the whole app**, not just its colours: the top
  bar, cards, buttons, fields, menus, dialogs, tabs, progress bars, type
  and the picture behind the page all take after what the theme is named
  for. The rest stay as they were.
  - Desktop 95: the top bar is a taskbar with the wordmark as its Start
    button, every page is a grey window with a blue title bar on a teal
    desktop, cards are group boxes, buttons are bevelled and press in,
    fields are sunken, and the scrollbars have arrow buttons.
  - Terminal: a green screen with glowing text and faint scan lines, a
    prompt and a blinking cursor in each page's title, boxes drawn in
    double lines and buttons in brackets. Terminal Light is the same on
    a paper-white screen.
  - Newsprint: a broadsheet, with the wordmark as a blackletter masthead
    over a dateline, headlines in a heavy serif, sections under rules,
    covers printed as halftones until you point at them and dialogs cut
    out like coupons. Newsprint Dark is its night edition.
  - Blueprint: an architect's drawing on squared paper, with hand
    lettering, dimension lines under the titles, corner marks on cards,
    cyanotype covers and a title block naming each page. Whiteprint is
    the same drawing in blue lines on white.
  - Notebook: an exercise book, in handwriting, with the red margin and
    punched holes, freehand boxes, books taped in and menus on sticky
    notes. Notebook Dark is a black notebook written in white.
  - Quest (in the Silly set): the menus of an old role-playing game,
    with blue windows in white frames, pixel letters, a gold pointer on
    whatever you're choosing and reading progress as an experience bar,
    under a starry sky.
  - Pinstripe (light): an early-2000s desktop, with grey pinstripes,
    glossy gel buttons (the main one blue), a menu bar, dialogs as
    windows with the red, amber and green lights in the corner (the red
    one closes it), glossy covers and progress bars whose stripes roll
    along. With an accent colour picked, the gel takes that colour.
  - Aero (new, light): Windows 7. The top bar is a taskbar of dark glass
    with a glowing Start orb, and every page is a window of sky-blue
    glass on a blue desktop with a burst of light: light catching in
    streaks across the frame, the page's name glowing in the caption,
    the minimise, maximise and red close buttons, and a white pane
    inside. Buttons are glossy and turn blue under the pointer, panels
    have blue titles, tabs stand up like Windows tabs, and progress bars
    are green with a shine that slides along them as the page opens.
    Dialogs and popovers are glass windows that blur what is behind them.
  - Golden Gate (new, light): macOS Golden Gate and its Liquid Glass.
    The app is a window of clear frosted glass, lit along its rim and
    casting a shadow, on a desktop of sweeping folded curves in sandy
    gold, soft grey and indigo; the gold and lavender show through it.
    A toolbar of glass floats across the top of the window, the covers
    glowing through it as they scroll under, with the open page as a
    brighter lens. Panels are panes of glass with a sheen of light
    across them, buttons are glass capsules that give a little when
    pressed (the main one in the system's blue), and menus and dialogs
    are glass over the page, dialogs with round, glossy traffic lights;
    the red one closes it. The page scrolls behind the window's rounded
    edges, as in a real window. Text on the glass keeps its contrast
    over any part of the picture and any cover.
  - KDE Plasma (new, light): Plasma 6.8 with Breeze. The app is a Breeze
    window on the Hanabi wallpaper, blurred fireworks in peach and rose
    over indigo and cornflower blue with one sharp spark. The window is
    light grey with white panels, a hairline round it and Breeze's large
    soft shadow, darkest underneath. Its title bar and toolbar are one
    grey band: the app's icon and the page's name above, thin minimise,
    maximise and close glyphs at the right, and the page links as flat
    toolbar buttons, the open page's checked. Plasma's panel floats
    below the window with the launcher, a few task icons (the app's own
    lit blue under a blue line) and the tray. Buttons are near-white
    with a darker line under them. The pointer shows as a crisp
    Breeze-blue edge and the keyboard as a thicker, deeper blue one,
    never a glow. Ticked boxes are pale blue round a dark tick, the open
    tab has a blue strip along its top, menus light their item in
    translucent blue, and dialogs are Kirigami sheets that dim only the
    window. The page scrolls behind the window's edges. The sign-in
    pages are Plasma's login screen: white type and a dark frosted sheet
    on the dimmed wallpaper.
  - GNOME (new, light): GNOME 51 with libadwaita. The Shell's black bar
    runs along the top of the screen, workspace dots at its left and
    status icons at its right. The app is a near-white window with round
    corners and a soft grey halo, on the Roundhex desktop of glassy
    hexagons in electric blue and violet. Its header bar is flat, with
    the page links as a view switcher in the middle (the open page a
    grey rounded fill) and the lone round close button at the far right.
    The library is one white boxed list with hairlines between its rows,
    and other cards are white with whisper shadows. Buttons are
    translucent grey with bold labels (the main one solid blue, a
    destructive one red on a pale red tint). Fields look like buttons
    and take a blue ring while you type. Tabs and shelves are toggle
    groups with the chosen one raised in white, switches and check boxes
    turn blue, and menus and popovers point at their button with GTK's
    tail. On a phone, dialogs rise from the bottom as sheets. An accent
    colour chosen in Preferences replaces the blue everywhere. The page
    scrolls behind the window's rounded edges, as in a real window.
  - Ubuntu (new, dark): Ubuntu's own desktop in its dark style, Yaru,
    on an aubergine desktop warming to orange with ribbons of light
    across it. The black top panel runs along the top of the screen
    with Activities and the status icons, and the Ubuntu Dock runs
    down the left: each page is an app with a coloured tile, the open
    one marked by an orange dot, and the other buttons sit at its foot
    over the Show Applications grid. Every page is a charcoal window
    with round corners and a header bar naming it, with the three
    round window buttons (the close one orange). Buttons are
    translucent grey (the main one Ubuntu's orange), fields take an
    orange ring while you type, the open tab is underlined in orange,
    progress fills in orange and everything is in Ubuntu Sans. On a
    phone the dock moves to the foot of the screen.
  - Hyprland (new, dark): the tiling Wayland compositor, set up the way
    its users rice it, in Catppuccin Mocha. There are no title bars,
    buttons or dock. The app is a tiled window: a bare dark rectangle
    whose 2px border runs from cyan at the top left to mint at the
    bottom right, set in even gaps on Hyprland's own wallpaper (a navy
    rain of capsules, contour lines and the glowing droplet logo). A
    Waybar floats above it in JetBrains Mono. The page links are
    numbered workspaces with the open one filled, the page's title sits
    in the middle as the window module shows it, and the icons on the
    right form a module of their own, each in its own pastel. Cards,
    library tiles and the bulk-edit bar are smaller windows with grey
    borders, and focus follows the mouse: the one under the pointer
    takes the glowing border, and keyboard focus adds Hyprland's glow.
    Menus, popovers and dialogs float as windows that pop in from a
    little smaller. A dialog takes the focus, so the app's window turns
    grey and dims, while the bar and the desktop stay lit. Buttons,
    pills and checkboxes follow the reader's Accent colour, and the
    window borders keep Hyprland's gradient. The page scrolls behind the
    window's edges, as in a real window. Signed out, the desktop is
    bare: the sign-in window floats beside the logo, which shows whole
    above the splash line "Read the wiki.", and on a phone the window
    covers the logo completely.
  - Fluent (new, dark): Windows 11 in its dark mode, Mica and acrylic.
    The app is a Windows 11 window on a wallpaper of our own, a bloom of
    silky violet and rose petals glowing on near-black. The window is
    Mica, dark and faintly tinted by the bloom behind it, with a title
    bar naming the page and thin minimise, maximise and close glyphs.
    The top bar becomes the taskbar along the foot of the screen, of
    acrylic that blurs what passes under it: the page links are centred
    icons, the open page's marked by a short lavender pill, and the rest
    sit in the tray at the right. Buttons are Windows' soft fills (the
    main one lavender), fields have a line along their foot that turns
    lavender while you type, the open tab has a pill under it, and menus,
    popovers and dialogs are acrylic; a dialog's close button turns red
    under the pointer. On a phone the window is maximised, with no title
    bar, and on the sign-in pages it stands alone on the desktop.
  - visionOS (new, dark): Apple Vision Pro. The app is a window of dark
    glass floating in front of a mountain valley (snowy ranges, a pine
    forest, a still lake), the valley showing through it blurred and
    dimmed, with very round corners and a light rim. The page links are
    an ornament beside the window: a capsule of glass with icons that
    shows their names when you point at it. The other buttons sit in a
    toolbar capsule on the window's foot, with the window bar and its
    close dot hanging below. Panels are lighter glass, buttons are glass
    capsules (the main one white), fields are pressed into the glass,
    check boxes are round, switches turn green and tabs are a segmented
    capsule. Menus, popovers and dialogs are glass over the page. On a
    phone the page links become a capsule along the foot of the screen.
    The page scrolls behind the window's rounded edges, and white text
    keeps its contrast over every part of the valley.
  - Porcelain (new, light): soft, unglazed porcelain, simple and calm.
    The page and everything on it are one pale cool grey, with no lines
    and no picture, shaped only by light falling from the upper left:
    panels, library tiles, buttons and the top bar stand up out of the
    page with a white edge and a soft shadow, while fields, the tab row
    and the open page's link are pressed into it. A button sinks in when
    you press it. Only the main buttons and what is chosen take colour,
    a raspberry glaze (or the accent colour you picked), and headings
    are in a rounded face. With flat cards picked, panels lie in the
    page with a hairline round them.
  - Graphite (new, dark): Porcelain in dark graphite, shaped by the same
    light, with a rose glaze for the main buttons and what is chosen.
  - Three looks of the Mac from the last ten years, each drawn fresh in
    its spirit (no Apple logo, none of Apple's pictures). In all three
